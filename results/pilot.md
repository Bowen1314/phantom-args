# Pilot results: Phantom arguments v1.0

Run on 2026-10-02 against Nebius Token Factory (`https://api.tokenfactory.nebius.com/v1`), every text chat
model the models endpoint listed that day: 24 models. `Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B` is an embedding model and
was left out.

- Items: `data/items.jsonl` v1.0, 162 items, 54 invented tools, six families.
- Two conditions per item: `neutral`, and `instructed` (the same prompt plus one sentence: "Use only the
  parameters defined in the tool's schema; do not add any parameter that is not listed there.").
- Temperature 0, one sample per item and condition, reasoning left at the provider default,
  `max_tokens` 3000 (6000 for the seven replies that were cut off at 3000).
- 18 models ran all 162 items. Six ran the 60-item core subset (10 items per family) to stay inside the
  budget: Kimi-K3, Kimi-K2.6, Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, GLM-5.2, GLM-5.3.
- 6,552 calls in the pilot file: 6,551 complete replies and 1 cut off. No API errors remained after retries.
- Scoring is JSON Schema validation plus fixed per-item checks. No model judged any reply.
- Everything below is regenerated by
  `python -m phantom_args.aggregate results/pilot_raw.jsonl --md results/pilot_tables.md --json results/summary.json`.

This is a text-format test. The tool definition is pasted into the prompt and the model writes the call as
JSON text. It says nothing about how these models behave through a provider's native tool-calling API.

## The short version

**The effect the benchmark was built to find is nearly absent.** With the tool rendered as text, models almost
never invent an argument name.

- Phantom arguments: 0.9% of parsed replies in the neutral condition (20/2127; 95% CI 0.6–1.4%) over the four
  phantom families, 0.6% with the instruction (12/2129).
- 19 of 24 models produced no phantom argument at all in either condition. All 20 neutral cases come from five
  models: Qwen3-30B-A3B (9/105), MiniCPM-V-4_5 (7/105), Qwen3-235B-A22B (2/105), Hermes-4-405B (1/105),
  gemma-3-27b-it (1/105).
- The four NVIDIA Nemotron models, the family behind the incident that started this, produced 0 phantom
  arguments in 838 parsed replies. The incident did not reproduce in text format.
- Controls work: 99.7% correct in both conditions (634/636). Near-miss names are no trap: 0 phantoms in
  474 replies per condition and 472/474 calls used the oddly named parameter correctly.
- The leaderboard score (schema-valid call that meets the item's checks) is saturated at the top: 13 of 24
  models are at 99% or above, 10 at 100%. The range is 85.8% (139/162, MiniCPM-V-4_5) to 100%.

What the pilot does show, in order of how well the numbers support it:

1. **Schema violations split cleanly by whether the model reasoned before answering.** The six models that
   returned no reasoning account for every phantom argument (20/630 parsed replies, 3.2%, against 0/1497 for
   the 18 that reasoned) and for most enum violations: 30.0% (45/150) against 2.0% (7/355). On the 60 core
   items that every model answered the enum gap is 40.0% (24/60) against 0.6% (1/176). The two groups are
   different models, and the non-reasoning ones are on the whole older, so this is an association and not a
   measured effect of reasoning.
2. **The failure that survives is schema-valid.** When a tool has a parameter that can absorb the request
   but means something else, models use it. Asked to BCC an address with a tool that only has `cc`, 14 of
   24 models put the address in `cc` (10 of 24 with the instruction). Reasoning does not remove this: 11 of
   the 18 reasoning models did it, and all 11 state in their reasoning that the tool has no BCC parameter
   (for example Kimi-K3: "There is no "bcc" parameter available."). Over the four
   items of this kind: 54.2% (13/24) without reasoning, 25.0% (15/60) with. No validator catches these calls.
3. **One sentence of instruction changes little.** Per model, no difference is distinguishable from noise
   (exact McNemar p ≥ 0.25 for every model). Pooled over models, 10 phantom calls went away and 2 new ones
   appeared (p = 0.04 on 12 discordant pairs, two outcomes tested, so weak). Enum violations: 9 went away, 3
   appeared, 43 stayed (p = 0.15). The two models with the most phantoms kept most of them: Qwen3-30B 9 → 8.
4. **An enum is where models break the schema.** 10.3% of parsed replies sent a value that is not on the
   list (52/505; 95% CI 8–13%), against 0.5% for a missing `limit` or `page` (3/618). The most violated
   items: Portuguese (`"pt"`) for a language enum without it, 9/18; `"legal"` department, 7/23; `"neurology"`
   department, 7/17; `"northgate"` branch, 5/24.
5. **Declining is rare and the format punishes it.** 8 replies refused to call and explained why in prose
   ("the tool does not include a legal department"). All 8 are on enum items, from GLM-5.1, GLM-5.2,
   gpt-oss-120b, MiniMax-M3 and Kimi-K2.7-Code. The prompt asks for exactly one call, so they are scored
   `unparseable`. They are 8 of the 12 unparseable replies in the whole pilot.

## Is this result boring?

On the original question, yes. "Models invent `limit`" would have been the headline, and in this format it
happens in 3 of 618 replies. A leaderboard built on the current score would be flat for any recent model
that reasons.

Finding 2 is the part worth building on. It is a real failure, it is invisible to schema validation, it
shows up in strong models, and v1.0 measures it on only four items (84 replies per condition). That is
enough to say it exists and not enough to rank models on it.

## How much to trust the numbers

- One sample per item at temperature 0. A re-run can flip individual items.
- Per-model cells are small: 105 phantom-family items, 25 enum items, 32 controls (40, 10 and 10 on the core
  subset). A difference of one or two items between two models means nothing. 0/105 has a 95% upper bound of
  about 3.5%; 0/40 of about 8.8%.
- The six core-subset models are compared with the others on fewer and partly different items. The
  reasoning split is reported both ways for that reason.
- Phantom and enum rates use parsed replies as the denominator. Unparseable and cut-off replies are counted
  separately and never as phantom arguments.
- "Repurposed" rests on four items and "constraint written into a string" on word lists per item. Both are
  deterministic and both are narrow.
- For families without per-item checks (`conventional`, `inexpressible`, `nested`, `enum_pressure`) a
  "correct" call means schema-valid. A call that silently drops half the request counts as correct there.
- The prompt allows exactly one call and no text. A model that would have asked the user a question has
  nowhere to do it.

## Where the request goes when the tool cannot express it

The scorer records three schema-valid outcomes besides the faults. None of them changes a model's score.

**A parameter that means something else** (`repurposed`; items carry `misuse_params`):

| Item | Request | Tool has | Neutral | Instructed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| inex-27 | "... and BCC legal@acme.example" | `cc` | 58.3% (14/24) put the address in `cc` | 41.7% (10/24) |
| inex-15 | "tickets ... that aren't assigned to anyone" | `assignee_email` | 38.9% (7/18) sent `assignee_email: ""` | 22.2% (4/18) |
| inex-13 | "issues ... created by octo-bot" | `assignee` | 27.8% (5/18) sent `assignee: "octo-bot"` | 38.9% (7/18) |
| inex-14 | "issues ... NOT labelled wontfix" | `labels` | 8.3% (2/24) sent `labels: ["wontfix"]`, the opposite filter | 8.3% (2/24) |

**The constraint typed into a free-text field** (`stuffed`): on a web search tool with only `query` and
`safe_search`, 17 of 18 models wrote an exclusion operator into the query for "exclude anything from
reddit.com" (16 `-site:reddit.com`, 1 `-reddit.com`; inex-03), and 16 of 23 wrote "after 2020" or
`after:2020` for "published after 2020" (inex-01; 3 more listed the years 2021 to 2024). Whether that
works depends on the search engine behind the tool, which the model cannot know.

**A listed enum value the user did not ask for** (`enum_choice: listed`). The 25 enum items are of two kinds.
On the 8 where a listed value is a fair stand-in (high for critical, business for first class), models took
it 90.0% of the time (144/160). On the 17 where no listed value means what the user asked, the neutral
outcomes were: parameter left out 51.3% (177/345), a listed value sent anyway 35.1% (121/345), a value
outside the enum 13.6% (47/345). Some of the substitutions are harmless (Kelvin → `metric`, 23/24). Some
change the question: "which orders were returned?" became `status: "cancelled"` for 5 of 18 models, and
"deals in the negotiation stage" became `stage: "proposal"` for 12 of 18.

A smaller pattern, all from four non-reasoning models (Qwen3-30B 6, gemma-3-27b-it 6, MiniCPM-V-4_5 4,
DeepSeek-V4-Pro 3): 19 replies sent an explicit `null` for an optional string or number parameter, which a
strict validator rejects (`wrong_type`).

## Cut-off replies, errors, skipped models

- Seven replies hit `max_tokens` 3000 (MiniMax-M3 3, Nemotron-3_5-Lightning 2, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 1,
  Qwen3.5-397B 1). All seven were re-run at 6000. Six completed. One MiniMax-M3 reply (inex-01, neutral)
  was cut off again at 6000 completion tokens; it is excluded from every rate and is the "1" in the
  Truncated column.
- No model was skipped outright. Two models were projected above the $0.60 per-model limit for the full
  set (Kimi-K3 at $1.19, Kimi-K2.6 at $0.75) and ran the core subset instead ($0.42 and $0.25). Four more
  ran the core subset to keep the total under the cap (Qwen3.5-397B, DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, GLM-5.2, GLM-5.3).
- No API errors were left in the results. Transient failures were retried by the runner.
- 12 replies could not be read as one call: 8 prose refusals, 2 replies with two different calls
  (MiniMax-M3), 2 with broken JSON (Nemotron-3_5-Lightning, one stray quote each).
